Option A — two separate cards per turn

YouExplain how neural networks learn, step by step
AssistantHow neural networks learn
At the heart of how neural networks learn is an optimization loop called gradient descent. The network first makes a prediction from the input, and a loss function measures how far that prediction is from the target. Backpropagation then walks backwards through the layers, computing how much every individual weight contributed to that error. Finally, gradient descent nudges each weight in the direction that reduces the loss, scaled by the learning rate — too large and training diverges, too small and it crawls. Repeating this loop over thousands of examples slowly shapes the network's internal features until its predictions become useful.
"…nudges each weight in the direction that reduces the loss, scaled by the learning rate…"
Go deeper on the learning rate — why does it make or break training?
AssistantThe learning rate
Gradient descent is best pictured as a hiker descending a foggy valley: the loss surface is the terrain, the current weights are your position, and the gradient is the direction of steepest ascent — so you step the opposite way. The learning rate is your step size: a good one lands you in the lowest valley, a bad one sends you bouncing between hillsides or freezing in place.
